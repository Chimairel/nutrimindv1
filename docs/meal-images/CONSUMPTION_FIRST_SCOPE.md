# Consumption-first image scope

## Correction

The primary image backlog should represent foods and meal combinations commonly
eaten by Filipinos, whether or not those foods originated in the Philippines.
The earlier Wiki Loves Food pool remains useful as a supplemental source, but it
must not define the product catalogue by itself.

## Evidence base

The DOST-FNRI 2021 Food Consumption Survey reports that 96.6% of Filipino adults
consumed well-milled rice, while 31.3% consumed egg. It also identifies pandesal,
instant noodles, lean kasim, galunggong, white chicken meat, bangus, tilapia,
hotdog, tuyong tamban, malunggay, kalabasa, talong, sitaw, okra, repolyo, carrot,
and sweet-potato leaves among commonly consumed foods.

The 2018-2019 adult survey gives a more detailed top-30 table. It reports rice at
94.8%, chicken egg at 28.1%, lean pork at 19.2%, pandesal at 13.0%, chicken meat
at 11.5%, galunggong at 10.6%, liempo at 9.9%, bangus at 8.2%, tilapia at 7.7%,
hotdog and pande monay at 6.9%, canned sardines at 6.8%, potato at 6.6%, and
frigate tuna at 6.1% of adults consuming the item on the survey day.

Sources:

- DOST-FNRI, *Philippine Nutrition Facts and Figures 2021 — Food Consumption Survey*: https://enutrition.fnri.dost.gov.ph/uploads/2021%20ENNS%20FandF%20Food%20Consumption%20Survey.pdf
- DOST-FNRI, *2018-2019 Facts and Figures — Food Consumption Survey*: https://enutrition.fnri.dost.gov.ph/uploads/2018-2019%20Facts%20and%20Figures%20-%20Food%20Consumption%20Survey.pdf

## Product interpretation

The surveys rank food items, not complete recipes. The generated 100-subject
backlog therefore labels its main entries `FNRI_ANCHORED_DERIVATION`: familiar
meal preparations are inferred from one or more measured consumption anchors.
That inference must not be presented as an official FNRI recipe ranking.

Examples include fried egg, boiled egg, scrambled egg, steamed rice, garlic
rice, instant noodles with egg, hotdog-egg-rice, chicken and rice, sardines and
rice, tuna sandwiches, oatmeal, and other globally familiar foods actually eaten
in the Philippines.

The six `SECONDARY_CONTEXTUAL` entries broaden urban/light-meal coverage but are
not claimed to appear in FNRI's top-food table.

## Image strategy

1. Source the 100 consumption-first photo subjects before expanding niche or
   heritage dishes.
2. Prefer an exact preparation image. Do not use a generic raw ingredient photo
   for a composed meal without the `Representative image` label.
3. Preserve the earlier 117 Philippine-dish candidates as a supplemental pool.
4. A failed remote thumbnail must render a deliberate preview-unavailable panel
   linking to the source page, never exposed alt text.
5. Cloudinary receives only visually approved assets with complete attribution.
